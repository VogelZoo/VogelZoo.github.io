From muscle signal to robot arm

Follow one EMG signal through six steps. Every live graph shows the same 10 seconds, with “now” at the right edge.

Simulated input

1Record the raw signal

Electrodes on the skin pick up the tiny voltage a muscle makes when it tightens. The raw signal is fast, jagged and messy.

2Filter out what isn’t muscle

A band-pass filter removes slow drift from cable and arm movement and fast electrical noise. A notch filter removes 60 Hz power-line hum. Compare the trace with step 1.

Blue shape: where the muscle signal’s energy lies. Black line: how much of each frequency the filters let through. Dashed pink: 60 Hz hum.

▼ the clean signal still swings positive and negative

3Rectify, then measure with RMS

Rectifying flips negative swings upward so every sample counts as activity. RMS then averages the signal over a short window into one smooth intensity (thick lines). A longer window is smoother but reacts later.

RMS squares the signal, so full-wave and off give the same intensity. Half-wave discards half the energy, so it reads lower.

5Turn intensity into a command

The controller subtracts triceps from biceps. A positive difference bends the elbow, a negative one straightens it, and the command follows smoothly.
